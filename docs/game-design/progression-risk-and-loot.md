# Progression, risk and loot

> Status: Approved principles with balancing details open
> Owner: Game Design Lead
> Last reviewed: 2026-07-20

## Progression hierarchy

Hunter Order separates three related concepts:

1. **Opportunity** is the core systemic resource created by the world.
2. **Skills** are the primary progression axis used to reach and exploit opportunities.
3. **Loot** is the secondary progression and economic outcome of successful pursuits.

Power is a consequence of exploration and capability, not the final purpose of the game.

## Exploration rewards

Play time alone must not determine value.

A Hunter who spends ten hours repeating one known farm route may gain predictable output, but a Hunter who spends two hours reading the world, entering an unknown region and accepting meaningful danger may create greater value.

Reward should correlate with:

- novelty;
- uncertainty;
- preparation;
- knowledge;
- distance from control;
- danger;
- competitive pressure;
- successful return.

Exact formulas are not approved.

## Anti-repetition rules

The game must resist dominant loops based on static repetition.

Approved constraints include:

- long exploitation changes regional conditions;
- resources and targets move or decline locally;
- dungeons cannot be repeated indefinitely as the dominant progression route;
- dungeon access and duration may be limited by in-world time or stability mechanics;
- daily login streaks must not become mandatory progression;
- multi-account advantages are prohibited;
- combat must support multiple viable builds rather than one universal solution.

## Unknown item state

A rare discovery may reveal its rarity before revealing its identity.

Before identification, the Hunter may not know:

- the item's name;
- function;
- market value;
- crafting relevance;
- historical relevance;
- whether it begins another pursuit.

Full identification requires transporting the item to an appropriate specialist, such as an archaeologist in a city.

## Transport and chase

A Hunter carrying an unidentified rare item can create a visible or discoverable risk signal. Other players may know the carrier has something rare without knowing what it is.

This supports meaningful decisions:

- intercept or ignore;
- travel alone or request protection;
- hide the route or move quickly;
- identify immediately or sell the mystery;
- share the discovery with a clan.

The exact tracking, visibility and PvP rules remain open.

## Defeat and item loss

Defeat must carry a high cost. It should create caution before unknown regions and make successful return part of the hunt.

The baseline loss model, protected equipment rules and PvP/PvE differences require dedicated balancing work.

## Merchant recovery event

After defeat, a random recovery event may occur:

> A merchant found the defeated Hunter and carried them to the nearest city. The wagon had limited free space.

Current rule:

1. The event has an unspecified chance to trigger.
2. The wagon recovers a random number of carried items from one to five.
3. Selection prioritizes item rarity.
4. Equal-rarity items are selected randomly.
5. The event reports which items were recovered.

Still unresolved:

- trigger probability;
- whether equipped items are eligible;
- rarity ordering details;
- whether destroyed, stolen and dropped items share one model;
- interaction with PvP interception;
- whether the merchant charges a fee;
- anti-exploit constraints.
