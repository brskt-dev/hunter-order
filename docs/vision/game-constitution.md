# Hunter Order game constitution

> Status: Approved
> Owner: Game Design Lead
> Last reviewed: 2026-07-20

> Hunter Order is a sandbox MMORPG where a living world constantly generates opportunities, and Hunters transform those opportunities into unique stories.

## Purpose

Hunter Order does not guide every player through the same authored story. It provides a persistent, systemic world in which creatures, resources, events, phenomena, expeditions and conflicts continuously create new opportunities.

The world does not exist to deliver one plot. It exists to produce circumstances worth pursuing.

A Hunter's progress is not measured only by attributes, equipment or hours played. Discovery, judgment, risk, knowledge, reputation and persistent impact are equally important.

## Player fantasy

The player is not a chosen hero destined to save the world. The player is a Hunter: a person who pursues something with intent, preparation and commitment.

A hunt may target:

- rare creatures;
- valuable resources;
- unknown artifacts;
- natural or supernatural phenomena;
- unexplored regions;
- information;
- contracts and rewards;
- alliances and strategic opportunities;
- rival Hunters.

The game must support multiple valid identities, including explorers, combatants, researchers, traders, producers, builders, farmers, cooks and clan leaders. No single path is the canonical way to play.

## Core player promise

The first emotional hook is curiosity:

> There is always something new to discover.

Long-term retention comes from two connected forces:

1. competition for scarce, rare or temporary opportunities;
2. social stories created by expeditions, alliances, rivalries, betrayals, trade and conflict.

The game should regularly produce community conversations such as:

- "Did you see what changed in that region?"
- "Someone found a creature nobody had catalogued."
- "Our expedition lost the route to another clan."
- "A controlled zone became wild again."

These events are the real content of Hunter Order.

## The core resource: opportunity

Opportunity is the systemic resource that connects the game.

- Exploration reveals opportunities.
- Skills determine which opportunities a Hunter can exploit.
- Loot is a consequence of successfully exploiting them.
- Rarity establishes prestige and competition.
- Conflict occurs when multiple Hunters value the same opportunity.

Skills are the primary progression axis. Loot is secondary. Neither replaces the deeper core: the ability to identify, reach, contest and exploit opportunities before they disappear or change.

## The world is independent

Players exist inside the world. The world does not exist to serve them.

It continues to evolve with or without a specific Hunter. Ecologies change, threats migrate, resources decline or recover, regions become controlled or return to wilderness, and servers accumulate different histories.

The player does not permanently master the world. The player learns to read it and adapt.

## Exploration over repetition

Hunter Order rewards meaningful exploration rather than raw session length.

Ten hours repeating one predictable action must not automatically be more valuable than two hours spent understanding signals, entering unknown territory and accepting real risk.

Repeated exploitation changes local conditions. A region may lose prey, attract predators, exhaust resources, alter migration patterns or become strategically uninteresting. The world should resist static farming routes and push Hunters toward new decisions.

## Tension as the default

Leaving controlled civilization should normally create tension. Exploration is not a harmless sightseeing activity. Unknown regions, creatures and rival Hunters must make preparation and return logistics meaningful.

This tension is balanced by rotating controlled zones, where sustained Hunter activity has driven away major hostilities. These zones are safer, not passive. Weaker creatures may remain neutral until attacked, and local conditions may deteriorate if control is abandoned.

## Rarity as prestige

Respect is tied to the rarity of what a Hunter has found, faced or accomplished, not only to numerical power.

A Hunter may earn recognition through:

- capturing or defeating an exceptionally rare target;
- discovering a region, species or phenomenon;
- recovering an unknown artifact;
- surviving an unusually dangerous expedition;
- leaving a persistent mark on the server's history.

The rarer the pursuit, the greater its social weight.

## Unknown discoveries

Rare items do not reveal their complete identity immediately.

A Hunter may know that an object is rare without knowing its name, function, market value or historical importance. Full identification requires reaching an appropriate specialist, such as an archaeologist or researcher in a city.

Transport creates uncertainty and emergent conflict. Other Hunters may know that a rare object is being carried without knowing whether interception is worth the risk. The carrier must decide whether to hide, sell, identify, protect or share the discovery.

## Meaningful defeat

Defeat must be expensive enough to preserve tension. It must not become a trivial reset.

Hunter Order also avoids arbitrary loss without world context. A recovery event may occur in which a merchant finds the defeated Hunter and transports them to the nearest city. The merchant has limited wagon space and can recover a random number of carried items from one to five.

Recovered items are selected by rarity, with random selection used to break ties between items of equal rarity. The exact event probability, eligibility rules and loss model require dedicated balancing specifications.

## Systemic naturalism

Every important system should have a plausible explanation inside the world.

Mechanics should appear to emerge from ecology, society, geography, logistics, history or institutions instead of exposing arbitrary game rules whenever possible.

Examples:

- a resource returns because the environment regenerated;
- a region becomes controlled because Hunters suppressed major threats;
- control is lost because wilderness reclaimed neglected territory;
- an artifact requires study because its properties are unknown;
- partial item recovery occurs because a merchant had limited transport capacity.

Abstraction is allowed. Unexplained arbitrariness is the design smell.

## What Hunter Order must resist

Hunter Order must not optimize itself around:

- indefinite farming of one static spawn;
- infinitely repeatable dungeons as the dominant progression loop;
- login obligations designed primarily to preserve streaks;
- multi-account advantages;
- progress determined only by hours played;
- one universally optimal combat build;
- a fixed mission path followed by every player;
- expansions as the only source of meaningful novelty.

At initial launch, one verified person maps to one Hunter account. In Brazil, the current identity rule is one CPF per Hunter. Any international identity model requires a separate decision.

Build sharing and specialization guides are valid community behavior, but the game must preserve multiple viable combat approaches and avoid a single solved build dominating all contexts.

## Design test

A proposed mechanic should be re-evaluated unless it strengthens most of these outcomes:

- creates new stories between players;
- produces or transforms opportunities;
- rewards exploration and interpretation;
- reinforces a living, independent world;
- preserves meaningful risk;
- supports multiple valid play styles;
- has a natural explanation inside the setting;
- leaves room for future change without erasing server history.

The world provides rules and opportunities. The stories belong to the Hunters.
